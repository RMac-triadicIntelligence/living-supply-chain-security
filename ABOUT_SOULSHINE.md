# About Soulshine

**soulshine** — (verb) the act of letting Jesus' light shine through you.

Soulshine is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit corporation based in Tulsa, Oklahoma, and
the licensor of the work in this repository.

## The ministry

Soulshine is a Christian young adult ministry for artists. The model is
borrowed deliberately from the Fellowship of Christian Athletes, founded in
1954, which uses athletics as its medium — Soulshine uses music, theater, and
the visual arts instead, and serves the students those programs reach:
vocal, theater, band, orchestra, and audiovisual, at the high school, college,
and young adult levels.

Students gather in small community groups, mentored by Christian teachers,
professors, and working artists. Groups study together, support each other, and
reach outward into their local communities. Art and music are global languages;
the vision is for local groups and regional festivals to grow from that.

## The self-funding difference

Most outreach ministries are donation-based and are not financially
self-supporting. A local chapter leader spends their time soliciting funding
from business owners and philanthropists.

Soulshine is built to fund its own ministry in each market by doing the thing
it exists to teach — hosting art shows, music festivals, and theatrical
productions, with an annual market-wide show highlighting every participating
school district. Proceeds support scholarships, community outreach, and music
and arts education.

The artists in the ministry, together with professionals volunteering their
time and talent, are what make outreach, fundraising, and community building
one activity rather than three.

## Why a research program sits under a ministry

Research and technology developed under the Soulshine umbrella is owned by the
nonprofit. Licensing that work commercially is a funding mechanism for the
charitable purpose above — the same principle as the festival model, applied to
a different kind of work.
